# Station Office and Programming

The Office is a late-1980s broadcast room with an angled stocked shelf, a
green blotter, and a staffed studio behind glass. The [perspective desk mockup](../web/public/mockups/perspective-desk.png)
records the approved composition direction. The busier room study guides
the edge props and atmosphere. See the [mockup library](mockups/README.md)
and [widget guide](office-widget-style.md).

## Playable office hub

The resting office keeps the central blotter clear. The phone, tins,
wall screens and background personnel provide atmosphere. Desk props and
the shelf open work. The active prop or workspace comes forward with the
rest of the room in view.

| Object | Opens |
| --- | --- |
| Stocked shelf | Owned catalogue canister, then the tape collection or series case |
| Desk calendar beside the phone | Programming and the Day, Week and Month views |
| Script folder under the lamp | Shared Ideas, Scripts and Production canister |
| Research binder | Survey orders and illustrated audience files |
| Incoming portfolio | Rights listings, Market dossier, assessments and industry wire |
| Ledger and calculator cluster | Station financial records |
| Review sheets | Transmission log and selected Airing file |
| Notes on the Today strip | Goals, next gap, unsold-ad reminders and timed Sway requests |

**Today** is a small single-channel display alongside the transport. It gives
a sense of where the station is in the day, takes tape drops and opens airing
files. It leaves multi-date arrangement and comparisons to the Programming
workspace. **Today** in Programming restores following the current period
after deliberate browsing. Day allows placement and moves. Week and Month
summarise first remaining gaps, airtime utilization, unsold minutes and the
latest scheduled instalment per recurring run. Dates outside the playable
window are marked. Month is a browsing view.

Pulling a tape puts it beside its two-leaf file. Closing the file puts the
physical tape away and preserves the selection. Browse by runtime and
production type and keep episodes in cases. The Brief shows a sketched
quality profile, Reception shows outlook and history, Members links known
credits, and Bottom Line records investment and returns. Label unknown and
inapplicable qualities explicitly. Tape, Airing and project files
keep their identity through contextual handoffs. Placement previews include
the station's default ad load and the exact interval. Clicking an airing
opens that airing's teal file, with offers before broadcast and cost and
settlement after. An inspected dated pre-air outlook can be retained locally
for comparison later. Visiting a completed airing does not invent one.

## Blotter layout and prop transitions

Preserve the clear central work surface. Keep differently shaped props
clustered along the edge with contact shadows rather than a uniform row
of links. Compact files use the
tabletop and dense tools use a larger office-framed overlay. Other props
remain visible and inert while a job is in focus.

An actionable prop reacts on hover or focus and shows its destination. A prop
can travel briefly to the blotter on activation, then return on close. Escape
can cancel during travel. Repeated activation opens one workspace and closing
restores focus. Reduced motion skips travel. Manual pause instead shows the
OFF AIR test card. Continue restores the open job and its state. The paused
clock and an off-air gap in the schedule are separate states. The station strip combines time controls, Today, the resource HUD and a
keyboard- and touch-operable Directory. The HUD uses icons with text tooltips and the fictional barred F for Funds. Keep
that mark recognisable as an F and provide a readable Funds label.

The broad desktop illustration has twelve local pose layers for office staff,
two operators and their studio cameras, a floor assistant and interview
participants. Each subject keeps its own deadline and can move while another
subject is moving. At a change it chooses a slight motion 72% of the time,
a gesture 21%, or the painted alternate posture 7%. Each tier has two
positions. Gestures and slight movements compose with either large pose.
The broad pose can stay in place until its next major change. Plant and light
have a separate slower cue. Narrow layouts keep the desk legible and omit
these distant character details.
The steam and wall displays have separate loops. Room motion can follow the
system, be enabled or be switched off. These timers affect presentation only.
The visual working day is 08:00–18:00 with cooler light and no steam outside it.
See [playtest direction](office-playtest-direction.md) for the separate proposal
for independent character motion and richer day transitions.

## Dossier stationery

| Folder | Colour | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tape | Taupe | A programme's qualities, public credits, reception and lifetime ledger |
| Airing | Teal | One showing's time, audience outlook, ad terms, result and settlement |
| Idea and Preparation | Pink family | Brief, assignment, writing or reporting, delivery and revision |
| Production | Light blue | People, rooms, exact costs, recording and completion |
| Character | Yellow | Public strengths, known commitments and contextual return |

Show two paper leaves even when one has little content. Use side tabs and
folded page corners to browse. Use typewriter copy and short handwritten notes,
with the artwork and working controls on separate layers. [Production
dossier](production-dossier.md) documents the live assignment and booking flow.

## Gameplay changes to explore separately

The office's visual working hours do not affect simulation availability.
A small transmission charge per occupied minute remains a proposal alongside
the current per-airing costs. Decide how it applies to short fillers, unsold
ad time and off-air intervals before adding it. Review wall-clock playback
speed independently of quarter-hour simulation inputs.

Lead times should follow the kind of work. The newsroom pilot repeats same-day
reporting, studio time and airtime, while films have their own development,
writing, filming and editing durations. Wider duration tuning, elastic
rentals and station action queues are separate decisions. See [production
logistics](production-logistics.md), [recurring newsroom](recurring-newsroom.md)
and [open questions](open-questions.md).
