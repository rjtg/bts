# Weekly advertiser reviews

Advertisers can adjust their bids after a week of paid, completed ad-minute
purchases. The industry wire and the advertiser's offer cards show the
direction of a change. The station sees neither the internal appraisal
measure nor the exact pricing adjustment.

The simulation reviews every seven days after that boundary's settlements.
Each advertiser needs two paid deals in the closing week. The reviewed
statistic is sampled total viewer-minutes divided by paid aired minutes.
Off-air releases do not count. Use the interval after the previous review
through the current boundary, inclusive of settlements at the boundary.

At or above 1,200 average viewers the brand becomes 10 percentage points
more willing to bid. Below 250 viewers it becomes 10 points less willing.
Between those thresholds its posture holds. The index starts at 100% and
stays between 75% and 125%. The adjustment scales willingness to pay before
normal offer limits and cash constraints. There are no transfers at review,
no cash replenishment, and no revaluation of signed deals or escrow.
Targeting and hype continue to affect exposure and demand separately.

The thresholds, step, and sample requirement are provisional. This pilot
uses station-viewing exposure as a proxy and has no evidence of conversion,
profit, or audience identity for the advertiser's review. The controlled
fixture starts with the same two signed purchases and changes only actual
viewing availability during delivery. Both sets of signed deals pay the
same price. After equalizing other quote inputs, the next offer is 987
Funds following strong delivery and 808 Funds following weak delivery.
