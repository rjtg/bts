# Weekly advertiser reviews

Advertisers can adjust their bids after a week of paid, completed ad-minute
purchases. The industry wire and the advertiser's offer cards show the
direction of a change. The station sees neither the internal appraisal
measure nor the exact pricing adjustment.

The simulation reviews every seven days after that boundary's settlements.
Each advertiser needs two paid deals in the closing week. The reviewed
statistic is sampled total viewer-minutes divided by paid aired minutes.
Off-air releases do not count. Use the interval after the previous review
through the current boundary, inclusive of settlements at the boundary.

At or above 1,200 average viewers the brand becomes 10 percentage points
more willing to bid. Below 250 viewers it becomes 10 points less willing.
Between those thresholds its posture holds. The index starts at 100% and
stays between 75% and 125%. The adjustment scales willingness to pay before
normal offer limits and cash constraints. There are no transfers at review,
no cash replenishment, and no revaluation of signed deals or escrow.
Targeting and hype continue to affect exposure and demand separately.

The thresholds, step, and sample requirement are provisional. This pilot
uses station-viewing exposure as a proxy and has no evidence of conversion,
profit, or audience identity for the advertiser's review. The controlled
fixture starts with the same two signed purchases and changes only actual
viewing availability during delivery. Both sets of signed deals pay the
same price. After equalizing other quote inputs, the next offer is 987
Funds following strong delivery and 808 Funds following weak delivery.

## Three-review comparison

Four histories start from the same station, cash, programmes and calendar.
They air the same programme on the final two evenings of each of three weeks.
The first two branches sell at the same starting terms and change only the
actual campaign. Creative quality falls to zero in one. In the turnout branch
the actual watchable fraction falls to 1% during delivery. The final branch
airs the same programme with no ad sales. Normal conditions are restored for
each new quote. None of these controls is a new player command.

| Campaign history | Mean main target hype after week three | Bid index | Total station receipts | Remaining budget | Quote for same next slot |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Normal | 28.69 | 125% | 3,030 | 16,970 | 248 Funds / 2 min |
| Weak creative | <0.01 | 125% | 6,747 | 13,253 | 1,292 Funds / 3 min |
| Weak turnout | 0.52 | 75% | 5,591 | 14,409 | 795 Funds / 3 min |
| Unsold | <0.01 | 100% | 0 | 20,000 | 1,034 Funds / 3 min |

Both weak campaigns earn more receipts in this fixture. The brand with weak
creative keeps its competitive posture because people still watched. The
cautious posture reduces offers after weak turnout, but the resulting deficits
have a larger effect on price. Withholding sales produces a stronger later
quote at the cost of every intervening receipt. Each payment remains within its
reserved amount and the brand has less cash after each paid deal.

The experiment leaves policy unchanged. It does establish a gap in incentives,
but the model owns creative quality at the brand and lacks measured delivery
that can distinguish an unexpected miss from a deliberately misleading pitch.
A stronger penalty would apply to both. The fixed diary neither measures the
value of preserving future demand beyond week three nor establishes season
balance. See the [full output](../contrib/next-season/brand-comparison.txt).
